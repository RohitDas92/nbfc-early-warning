A tenure extension lengthens the repayment period, reducing the monthly
instalment.

A tenure extension may be granted between **thirty and ninety days past due**
inclusive and is authorised by a **manager**. **It is forbidden while the account
is in moratorium** — there is no instalment to reduce before repayment has begun,
and a borrower still in moratorium who needs relief is handled under section 2.1.

The lower bound is deliberate. Below thirty days past due the account has not
demonstrated a sustained affordability problem, and a payment date change under
Collections SOP 3.3 is the lighter and more likely correct response.

The upper bound is equally deliberate. Past ninety days the account is a
non-performing asset and extending its tenure does not make it performing; under
Collections SOP 2.2 only payment of the entire arrears upgrades an account.

The extension must produce an instalment the borrower can demonstrably afford.
An extension that leaves the instalment at a level the borrower has already
failed to pay is not a restructuring but a deferral of the same failure, and must
be refused.

Arrears outstanding at the date of extension do not disappear. They are either
cleared at the time of the extension or scheduled explicitly, and the treatment
must be stated in writing to the borrower.
