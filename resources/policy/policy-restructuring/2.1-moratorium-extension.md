A moratorium extension defers the start of principal repayment beyond the date
in the original sanction.

An extension may be granted at up to **thirty days past due** and is authorised
by a **manager**. Beyond thirty days past due it is not available, and the
account is assessed under section 2.2 or under the collections process.

The extension is appropriate where the borrower's course has been extended, where
placement has been delayed beyond the assumption in the original sanction, or
where the borrower has a confirmed start date that falls after the scheduled
repayment start.

Evidence is required: a letter from the institution for a course extension, or an
offer letter with a start date for a placement delay. A verbal assurance is not
evidence, and an extension granted without evidence on file is a policy breach
even where the borrower's circumstances were genuine.

An extension alters when principal repayment begins. It does not waive interest
accrued during the extended period, and it does not alter the classification of
an account that was already delinquent when the extension was granted.

Where the sanction names the co-applicant as the servicing party during
moratorium, that obligation continues through the extension and must be restated
in writing when the extension is granted.
