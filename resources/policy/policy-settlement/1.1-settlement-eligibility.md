A settlement is an agreement to accept less than the full contractual dues in
final discharge of the loan.

A settlement requires **more than ninety days past due**, that is ninety-one days
or more. It requires that the account has already been handed over to the
collections team under Collections SOP 5.1. **It is forbidden while the account
is in moratorium.**

The ninety-one day threshold is deliberately set past the point at which the
account becomes a non-performing asset under Collections SOP 2.1. Settlement is
not a servicing tool for a merely delinquent account; where the borrower can
resume paying, the restructuring options under the Restructuring Policy are the
correct route and must be considered first.

Approval authority for a settlement is not fixed by this section. It follows the
waiver being sought, under the approval matrix in section 2. A settlement whose
waiver has no authorised approver is refused, however small the shortfall.

A settlement must be justified in writing on the account file, stating what
recovery is realistically expected without it, and what has already been
attempted. An expected-recovery figure that is not supported by evidence on the
file is not a justification.

A settled account is reported to credit information companies as settled, not as
closed. Staff must not represent otherwise to the borrower.
