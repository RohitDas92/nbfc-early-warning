The authority approving a settlement must be independent of the authority that
sanctioned the loan.

Paragraph 16(5)(i) of the RBI (NBFC – Resolution of Stressed Assets) Directions,
2025 requires that the power to approve a compromise settlement rests with an
authority — individual or committee — **at least one level higher in hierarchy**
than the authority vested with power to sanction the credit exposure.

It further provides that any official who was part of sanctioning the loan,
whether as an individual or as part of a committee, **shall not be part of
approving the compromise settlement of that same loan account, in any capacity**.

Two consequences for this portfolio.

First, the waiver slabs in section 2 set a floor, not the answer. A waiver of
Rs 20,000 falls to a manager under section 2.1, but if a manager sanctioned the
loan then the approval must rise at least one level regardless of the amount.

Second, "in any capacity" is absolute. The original sanctioner may not vote, may
not chair, may not be co-opted as a subject expert, and may not be counted toward
quorum. Providing factual information about the account on request is not
participation in the approval; being in the room when it is decided is.

The approval record must name the sanctioning authority and the approving
authority, so independence is auditable after the fact rather than asserted at
the time.
