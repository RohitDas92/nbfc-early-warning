A technical write-off removes a non-performing asset from the books at the head
office level while the company continues to pursue recovery rights against the
borrower in full.

A write-off is a recommendation of the Legal team and a decision of the Board or
its delegate. It is never a collections decision and never a substitute for the
principal waiver that Settlement Policy 2.4 prohibits — the debt is not
extinguished and the borrower is not released.

Paragraph 16 of the RBI (NBFC – Resolution of Stressed Assets) Directions, 2025
requires a Board-approved policy covering technical write-offs, including the
conditions precedent such as minimum ageing and deterioration in collateral
value, a graded framework for examining staff accountability, and the delegation
of powers for approval.

Under paragraph 16(5)(i) the approving authority must be at least one level
higher in hierarchy than the authority that sanctioned the exposure, and no
official who was part of sanctioning the loan may take part in approving the
write-off in any capacity.

A recommendation states what recovery has been attempted, what remains legally
available, and why continued pursuit is not expected to recover the balance.
"Uneconomic to pursue" is a conclusion, not a reason, and must be supported.

This section is restricted to the Legal and Finance departments.
