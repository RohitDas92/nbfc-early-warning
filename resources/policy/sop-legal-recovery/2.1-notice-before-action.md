A demand notice is issued to the borrower and every co-applicant before any
legal proceeding is commenced.

The notice states the amount claimed, broken into principal, interest and
charges; the period over which it accrued; the contractual basis of the claim;
and a period of not less than fifteen days within which payment or a
representation may be made.

Charges shown in the notice must separate penal charges from interest.
Collections SOP 6.2 forbids capitalising penal charges, and a notice that shows a
merged figure both misstates the claim and evidences a breach of that rule in
writing.

Notice is served at the address recorded on the loan, by a method that produces
proof of dispatch and, where available, proof of delivery. Service on one
borrower is not service on a co-applicant; each is served separately.

A representation received within the notice period must be considered and
answered in writing before proceedings begin. Proceeding while a representation
is unanswered is the most common procedural defect raised against lenders and the
easiest to avoid.

Where the borrower disputes the amount, proceedings are held and the account is
returned to collections for reconciliation under section 1.1.
