A payment mandate authorises the company to debit the borrower's bank account
for the monthly instalment. Registration is completed before the first
instalment falls due.

Mandates are registered electronically through NACH, using either the Aadhaar or
net-banking authentication route, or by physical form where the borrower's bank
does not support the electronic route. The electronic route is preferred because
it fails fast and visibly; a physical form can sit unregistered for weeks.

Registration must capture the borrower's own operative bank account. A mandate
against a third party's account, including a parent who is not a co-applicant, is
not acceptable and must be rejected at source.

The registered mandate amount must be at least the instalment amount. Where the
sanction provides for a step-up schedule, the mandate is registered for the
highest scheduled instalment, so that the mandate does not have to be replaced
mid-tenure.

A mandate is active only once the sponsor bank confirms registration. Until that
confirmation is received the account has no working auto-debit, and the borrower
must be told in writing that payment remains their responsibility for the coming
cycle. Assuming a mandate is live because a form was submitted is the most common
cause of an avoidable first-instalment default on this portfolio.

Rejection reasons must be recorded verbatim, not summarised, because the reason
code determines whether repair under Collections SOP 3.2 will help.
