A bounce is a failed presentation. Bounces are classified as technical or
financial, and the classification determines the response.

**Technical** bounces arise from mandate data, signature mismatch, an inactive
mandate or a sponsor-bank error. They indicate a broken instruction, not an
unwilling or unable borrower. The response is mandate repair under Collections
SOP 3.2. A technical bounce must not by itself trigger collections escalation.

**Financial** bounces arise from insufficient funds or a debit-frozen account.
They indicate a capacity or willingness problem and the account follows the
normal collections process from section 3.1.

Misclassifying a technical bounce as financial is the more damaging of the two
errors: it puts a performing borrower into a collections queue and can produce a
field visit against an account that was never unwilling to pay.

Any charge levied for a bounce is a penal charge under RBI/2023-24/53 and is
governed by Collections SOP 6.2. It is not interest, it is never capitalised, and
it is communicated to the borrower at the time of levy.

Three consecutive financial bounces are an early warning signal in their own
right and must be raised for review irrespective of the days-past-due figure,
which may still be low if the borrower is paying manually after each failure.
