Days past due, or DPD, is the number of days for which any amount contractually
due on a loan has remained unpaid. DPD is counted from the due date, not from the
date the shortfall was noticed.

Classification into Special Mention Account categories follows paragraph 18 of
the RBI (NBFC – Resolution of Stressed Assets) Directions, 2025:

| Category | Principal or interest or any other amount wholly or partly overdue |
|---|---|
| SMA-0 | up to 30 days |
| SMA-1 | more than 30 days and up to 60 days |
| SMA-2 | more than 60 days and up to 90 days |

Paragraph 19 of the same Directions confirms that SMA classification applies to
all loans including retail loans, irrespective of the size of the exposure.

An asset becomes a non-performing asset where the instalment or interest has
remained overdue for more than ninety days, under paragraph 51 of the RBI (NBFC
– Income Recognition, Asset Classification and Provisioning) Directions, 2025.

Timing is governed by the IRACP Directions. Paragraph 18 requires the account to
be flagged overdue as part of the day-end process **for the due date**,
irrespective of when that process is actually run. Paragraph 19 requires SMA and
NPA classification to be performed as part of the day-end process for the
relevant date, with the classification date being the calendar date for which
the day-end process is run.

Illustration I in paragraph 19 is the reference case staff should use. For an
instalment due on 31 March 2021 where full dues are not received, the date of
overdue is 31 March 2021. The account is tagged SMA-1 on running the day-end
process on 30 April 2021, SMA-2 on 30 May 2021, and NPA on 29 June 2021.

Staff must not compute DPD by subtracting the due date from today's date on an
ad hoc basis; that produces a figure which disagrees with the system of record.
