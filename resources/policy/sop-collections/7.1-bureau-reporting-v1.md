Delinquency on this portfolio is reported to credit information companies on a
fortnightly cycle.

Under section 6(1)(b) of the RBI (Credit Information Reporting) Directions,
2025, credit information is kept updated on a fortnightly basis — as on the
fifteenth and the last day of each month — and submitted to credit information
companies within seven calendar days of the reporting fortnight. Section 16(6)
requires the credit information company to ingest the data received into its
database within five calendar days of receipt.

Two consequences follow, and collections staff must understand both.

First, a bureau score is a fact as at its pull date and may lag the account's
current position by up to roughly three weeks — a fortnight to the reference
date, seven days to submit, five to ingest. A score that does not yet reflect a
recent payment is not necessarily wrong, and a borrower who says they have paid
may be telling the truth about an event the bureau has not yet received.

Second, reporting a default is not a silent internal act. Section 16(1) requires
the credit institution to send an SMS or email alert to the customer when
submitting information regarding default or days past due. The borrower is told.

Staff must not use bureau reporting as a threat to induce payment. It is a
regulatory obligation performed on a fixed cycle, not a discretionary consequence
that can be applied or withheld, and describing it as one is a false
representation under section 4.2.

A borrower disputing reported information must be directed to the grievance
process, which must resolve the complaint within thirty calendar days.
