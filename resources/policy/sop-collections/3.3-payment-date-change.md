A payment date change moves the monthly instalment due date to a date better
aligned with the borrower's income cycle.

A payment date change may be granted at up to sixty days past due and is
authorised by the analyst handling the account. **It may not be granted beyond
sixty days past due.** Past that point the account requires a restructuring
decision under the Restructuring Policy, taken by a manager, rather than a
scheduling adjustment taken by an analyst.

The change is appropriate where payment failures cluster immediately before a
salary or stipend credit and resolve immediately after. It is not appropriate
where the borrower is unable to pay at any point in the month; that is a capacity
problem and a date change will not fix it.

A payment date change alters the schedule only. It does not reduce the amount
due, does not waive any charge, and does not alter the classification of the
account. Any accrued penal charge remains payable, and under RBI/2023-24/53
paragraph 3(i) is never capitalised into the principal.

The existing mandate must be cancelled and a fresh mandate registered for the new
date. Until the new mandate is active, the borrower must be told in writing that
payment remains their responsibility and that no auto-debit will be presented.
