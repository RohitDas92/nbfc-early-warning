Outsourced recovery agents act for the company and are bound by this SOP in
full. Nothing in an agency agreement can relax a rule in section 4.

Paragraph 17 of the RBI (NBFC – Managing Risks in Outsourcing) Directions, 2025
states that outsourcing shall not diminish the NBFC's obligations to its
customers or to the Reserve Bank, that the Board and senior management retain
ultimate responsibility for the outsourced activity, and that the NBFC is
responsible for the actions of its service providers including Direct Sales
Agents, Direct Marketing Agents and recovery agents, and for the confidentiality
of customer information held by them. The NBFC retains ultimate control.

Paragraph 98 of the RBI (NBFC – Responsible Business Conduct) Directions, 2025
requires agents to be properly trained to handle their responsibilities with care
and sensitivity, naming hours of calling and privacy of customer information
among the aspects to be covered. Paragraph 99 requires the company to obtain each
agent's undertaking to abide by the code of conduct, and requires agents to
observe strict customer confidentiality. Paragraph 97 requires up-to-date details
of engaged recovery agencies to be hosted on the company's website.

Every agent deployed on this portfolio must therefore be named on the current
published list, must carry company-issued identification on every visit, and must
have completed conduct training covering section 4 before deployment. An agent
not on the list has no authority to act.

Every contact made by an agent must be logged in the same contact history as
internal contact, with the same fields. Agency-held records not reflected in the
system of record do not satisfy this requirement and cannot prove a precondition
was met.

Any borrower complaint alleging conduct covered by section 4.2 must be recorded,
escalated to the Head of Collections within one working day, and the agent
suspended from the portfolio pending review.
