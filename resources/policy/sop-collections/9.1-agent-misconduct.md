A credible allegation that a recovery agent has breached Collections SOP 4.2
results in immediate suspension of that agent from the portfolio, pending review.

Suspension is not a finding of guilt and is not a disciplinary act against the
agency. It is a control: while an allegation of intimidation or harassment is
open, the company cannot allow the person complained of to continue contacting
borrowers on its behalf.

The review establishes what contact occurred, from the contact history and the
agency's own records, and whether it fell within the hours in section 4.1 and the
conduct rules in section 4.2. The complainant is contacted and their account
recorded verbatim.

A substantiated breach results in removal of the individual from the authorised
list and, where the agency's supervision is found wanting, termination of the
agency engagement. Paragraph 97 of the RBI (NBFC – Responsible Business Conduct)
Directions, 2025 requires the up-to-date list of engaged recovery agencies to be
published on the company's website, so a removal is visible externally and the
list must be corrected promptly.

Paragraph 100 makes the company responsible for ensuring that neither it nor its
agents engages in the prohibited conduct. A pattern of substantiated complaints
against one agency is a failure of the company's own oversight, not only the
agency's.

This section is restricted. Allegations, reviews and outcomes concern named
individuals and must not be discussed outside the review.
