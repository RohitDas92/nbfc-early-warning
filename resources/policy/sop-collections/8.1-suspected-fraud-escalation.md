Where collections activity gives rise to a suspicion of fraud, the account is
escalated to the Fraud Risk Management unit and **all collections action on it
stops immediately**.

Indicators include an institution that denies the borrower was ever enrolled,
identity documents that do not match on re-verification, a disbursement account
that does not belong to the named borrower, and a cluster of accounts sharing an
address, a device or a bank account.

On escalation the analyst stops contact, stops any scheduled presentation, and
does not tell the borrower that a fraud review is under way. Continuing to pursue
recovery on an account under fraud review contaminates the investigation and may
prejudice the company's position.

The RBI (NBFC – Fraud Risk Management) Directions, 2026 require NBFCs in the
Upper Layer and Middle Layer to maintain a framework for Early Warning Signals
(paragraph 13), and to identify appropriate early warning indicators for
monitoring credit facilities and loan accounts, with suspicion of fraudulent
activity thrown up by one or more EWS indicators triggering further examination
(paragraph 15). This company is Middle Layer, so the framework is mandatory.

Paragraph 6(1) requires a detailed Show Cause Notice to the persons, entities and
their promoters or whole-time and executive directors against whom an allegation
of fraud is being examined, giving complete details of the transactions, actions
or events on which declaration and reporting of fraud is contemplated. Reaching a
conclusion before that process has run is not open to the company.

This section is restricted. Its contents must not be discussed with the borrower,
with an outsourced agent, or with staff outside the Fraud Risk Management unit
and the Head of Collections.
