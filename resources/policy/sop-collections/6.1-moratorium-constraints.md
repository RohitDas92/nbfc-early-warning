While an education loan is within its sanctioned moratorium period, the
following actions are forbidden regardless of days past due, approval level or
amount outstanding:

- handover to the collections team (section 5.1)
- field visit (section 5.2)
- tenure extension (Restructuring Policy 2.2)
- settlement (Settlement Policy 1.1)

The moratorium is a term of the sanction. During it the borrower is not
contractually required to service principal, and in most structures not to
service interest either. An amount that is not yet due cannot be overdue, and a
recovery action premised on delinquency has no basis.

Where the sanction names the co-applicant as the servicing party during
moratorium, the co-applicant is the correct point of contact for any amount that
genuinely is due, and the borrower must not be pursued for it.

Any DPD figure shown against an account in moratorium must be treated as
suspicious and investigated as a possible data defect before any action is taken
on it. A non-zero DPD during moratorium is more often a booking error than a
default.

Permitted during moratorium: soft contact for information and relationship
purposes, mandate repair in preparation for the repayment start date, and
moratorium extension under Restructuring Policy 2.1. None of these is a recovery
action.
