Delinquency on this portfolio is reported to credit information companies four
times a month.

Under paragraph 6(2) of the RBI (NBFC – Credit Information Reporting)
Directions, 2025, as substituted with effect from 1 July 2026, a credit
institution submits credit information as on the **9th, 16th, 23rd and last day
of the month** — the reference dates. The full file, covering all active
accounts, is submitted as on the last day of the month by the **5th day of the
following month**. For the other three reference dates only incremental accounts
are furnished, within **four calendar days** of the reference date.

This replaces the earlier fortnightly cycle. Staff who learned the old 15th and
month-end pattern must unlearn it.

Two consequences follow, and collections staff must understand both.

First, a bureau record is a fact as at its reference date, not as at today. The
lag is now short — four days for an incremental submission, and up to eleven for
the month-end full file — but it is never zero. A borrower who says they have
paid may be telling the truth about an event the bureau has not yet received.

Second, reporting a default is not a silent internal act. Paragraph 34(1)(i)
requires the credit institution to send an SMS or email alert to the customer
when submitting information to credit information companies regarding default or
days past due, wherever a mobile number or email address is held. The borrower is
told.

Staff must not use bureau reporting as a threat to induce payment. It is a
regulatory obligation performed on a fixed cycle, not a discretionary consequence
that can be applied or withheld, and describing it as one is a false
representation under section 4.2.

A borrower disputing reported information must be directed to the grievance
process. Paragraph 35(1) entitles a complainant to compensation of ₹100 per
calendar day where resolution is delayed beyond the prescribed period.
