Cure and upgrade are different things and must not be used interchangeably.

An account is described internally as **cured** when its DPD has returned to zero
and remained there. This is an operational description used for portfolio
reporting and for measuring the effect of collections actions. It carries no
regulatory meaning.

**Upgrade** is a regulatory act. Under paragraph 24 of the RBI (NBFC – Income
Recognition, Asset Classification and Provisioning) Directions, 2025, a loan
account classified as an NPA may be upgraded to standard only if the entire
arrears of interest and principal are paid by the borrower. Partial payment does
not upgrade an account, and bringing DPD below ninety days does not upgrade an
account.

Paragraph 25 extends this where a borrower holds more than one facility: the
account is upgraded only upon repayment of the entire arrears of interest and
principal **pertaining to all the credit facilities**. Clearing one loan while
another remains in arrears upgrades neither, because paragraph 23 makes asset
classification borrower-wise rather than facility-wise.

Upgrade after restructuring is a separate regime. Paragraph 22 of the IRACP
Directions states that an asset shall not be upgraded merely as a result of
rescheduling unless it satisfies the conditions in the RBI (NBFC – Resolution of
Stressed Assets) Directions, 2025.

Staff must not describe an account as upgraded, regularised or standard in any
communication with a borrower, a bureau or an auditor unless the applicable test
has been met. Outcome labels produced by internal analytics, including any label
generated by the early warning system, are operational descriptions only and are
never a statement of asset classification.
