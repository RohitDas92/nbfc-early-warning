Wilful default is a finding about a borrower's conduct, not a description of
their arrears. A borrower who cannot pay is not a wilful defaulter; a borrower
who can pay and does not, or who diverts the funds, may be.

Collections staff do not make this finding. Where the indicators below appear,
the account is referred to the Wilful Default Identification Committee and
collections continues unchanged in the meantime.

Indicators: the borrower has the capacity to pay and has not; funds have been
diverted to a purpose other than the sanctioned one; the education for which the
loan was taken was never commenced or was abandoned while funds were retained;
or the borrower has disposed of assets given as security without consent.

The consequences of a wilful default finding are severe and long-lasting,
including exclusion from further credit across the system. The Reserve Bank's
directions on treatment of wilful defaulters require an identification process,
a show cause opportunity and a review mechanism before any such classification.
Staff must not anticipate the outcome.

Above all, staff must never tell a borrower they will be "declared a wilful
defaulter" to induce payment. It is a false and misleading representation under
Collections SOP 4.2, and it misdescribes a process the collections team does not
control.
